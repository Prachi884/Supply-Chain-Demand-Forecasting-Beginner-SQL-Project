# Project Findings — Supply Chain Demand Forecasting (Beginner Edition)

*This file walks through all 10 business questions in
`analysis_and_forecasting.sql`, in order, using the real results of
running each query against the full 100,000-row dataset (Jan 2023 –
Dec 2024, 20 stores, 150 products). Every number below is a direct
query output — nothing is illustrative.*

---

## Headline numbers

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Total revenue (2 years) | **$112.8M** |
| Overall stockout rate | **26.1%** of store-product-days |
| Transactions on promotion | **10.0%** of all transactions |

The dataset's core story: roughly **1 in 4 store-product-days run out
of stock before demand is met**. Every question below chips away at
*where* that's happening and *what else* is going on around it.

---

### Q1. "Which product categories generate the most total revenue?"

| Category | Revenue |
|---|---|
| **Electronics** | **$48.72M** |
| Home & Garden | $23.41M |
| Apparel | $13.90M |
| Toys | $13.04M |
| Health & Wellness | $9.46M |
| Grocery | $4.31M |

Electronics alone brings in more revenue than the bottom four
categories combined. That single fact shapes almost everything else
in this report — anywhere Electronics shows up as a problem later
(stockouts especially), it matters more than a similar-sized problem
anywhere else in the catalog.

### Q2. "What are our top 10 best-selling products by units sold?"

Whole Milk 1gal (37,334 units), Muffins 4pk (37,218), Coffee Beans
(29,940), Eggs 12ct (27,088), Robot Toy (26,036), Tortillas (25,845),
Cheddar Cheese (24,807), Butter (24,036), Potato Chips (24,013),
Trail Mix (23,073).

This list is **entirely Grocery**, except for one Toy — and Grocery is
the *lowest*-revenue category from Q1. High volume, low price, low
revenue-per-unit: these products keep people walking through the
door, but Electronics is what pays the bills.

### Q3. "How many stores do we have in each region?"

West: 5, South: 5, Northeast: 5, Midwest: 5 — an even split. Nothing
dramatic here, but it matters for later questions: when Q4 and Q9
show one region underperforming, it isn't because that region simply
has fewer stores to compare against.

### Q4. "Which 10 stores experienced the most stockout days?"

| Store | Region | Stockout days |
|---|---|---|
| Midwest Ohio #015 | Midwest | 1,410 |
| Midwest Illinois #012 | Midwest | 1,382 |
| West California #004 | West | 1,372 |
| West Arizona #002 | West | 1,355 |

Two of the top five are in the Midwest. That's an early hint — well
before Q9 confirms it with actual revenue numbers — that something
regional is going on, not just a product-level forecasting issue.

### Q5. "What is the average selling price per category?"

| Category | Avg unit price |
|---|---|
| Electronics | $409.97 |
| Home & Garden | $130.56 |
| Apparel | $48.17 |
| Toys | $41.46 |
| Health & Wellness | $32.82 |
| Grocery | $9.04 |

Electronics costs over **3x** the next-priciest category and **45x**
Grocery. This is the mechanical reason behind Q1 and Q2: Electronics
doesn't need to sell many units to dominate revenue — a handful of
$400+ sales outweighs thousands of $9 Grocery sales.

### Q6. "How many transactions happened during promotions vs. regular price?"

89,966 regular-price transactions moved 1,639,705 units. 10,034
promotional transactions moved 275,660 units. That means the **10% of
transactions running a promotion accounted for 14.4% of all units
sold** — promotions are punching above their weight in volume, even
at a small share of total transactions.

### Q7. "What did demand look like during the 2023 holiday season (Nov–Dec)?"

| Category | Demand | Sold |
|---|---|---|
| Toys | 59,276 | 55,218 |
| Grocery | 48,430 | 45,134 |
| Apparel | 47,916 | 44,520 |
| Health & Wellness | 29,240 | 26,951 |
| Electronics | 24,296 | 22,448 |
| Home & Garden | 18,002 | 16,355 |

Toys leads demand during this exact two-month window — no surprise,
since that's the seasonal spike it's built around. Worth noting:
Electronics ranks lower here in raw *demand* even though it dominates
*revenue* year-round (Q1) — a reminder that a category can be a top
revenue driver without being the top mover in units, even during its
own peak season.

### Q9. "What is the total revenue and profit generated by each region?"

| Region | Revenue | Profit |
|---|---|---|
| Northeast | $32.40M | $10.72M |
| West | $28.58M | $9.48M |
| South | $28.39M | $9.37M |
| **Midwest** | **$23.47M** | **$7.73M** |

Midwest trails every other region on both revenue and profit — and
this is exactly the region flagged back in Q4 for having two of the
worst-stockout stores in the company. That's a consistent regional
pattern showing up in two independent queries, not a coincidence.

### Q10. "Which 10 products had the highest number of stockout days?"

| Product | Category | Stockout days |
|---|---|---|
| Wireless Controller | Electronics | 238 |

| 55-inch 4K TV | Electronics | 217 |

**8 of the 10 worst-stockout products are Electronics.** Paired with

"fix this first" signal in the entire dataset — the category driving
almost half the business is also the one most often unavailable to
sell.

---

## Bottom line — three things this data says to fix first

1. **Fix Electronics availability, not overall inventory.** It's 43%
   of revenue and 8 of the 10 worst-stockout products in the whole
   catalog (Q1, Q10). Closing that gap matters more than an
   across-the-board inventory increase.
2. **Investigate the Midwest specifically.** It underperforms on
   revenue and profit (Q9) *and* shows up twice in the worst-stockout
   store list (Q4) — a sign this may be a regional
   allocation/replenishment issue, not just a demand problem.
3. **Lean into promotions, carefully.** They already drive 14.4% of
   units sold from just 10% of transactions (Q6) — but combining that
   with the Electronics stockout problem (Q10) means promoting
   Electronics specifically could make availability worse, not better.
